# Reviewer Response Draft

## Concern: This only repackages ECDC annual reports.

ECDC provides the authoritative surveillance totals. The added contribution is
a frozen, reproducible benchmark that links those totals to public covariates,
documents source-quality metadata, evaluates probabilistic retrospective
one-year-ahead predictions, and reports whether covariates improve over simple
surveillance baselines.

## Concern: Prior work already mapped hantavirus risk in Europe.

We agree and cite that literature, especially Zeimes et al. The present study
differs by evaluating temporal country-year reported incidence with a frozen
benchmark, public covariate joins, calibration, sharpness, WIS, and feature
ablation.

## Concern: Why not use county or district data?

The study intentionally benchmarks what free public cross-national human
surveillance can support. More granular human data are often privacy-limited;
CDC explicitly withholds U.S. county-level hantavirus data to protect
identities. We do not make county-level human-risk claims.

## Concern: Climate and land-use results are weak.

That is a core result, not a failure to hide. Sparse annual country-level
surveillance, reporting heterogeneity, and only five ECDC years make simple
baselines difficult to beat. The manuscript includes sensitivity and
detectability analyses to avoid over-interpreting null or mixed covariate
findings.

## Concern: The maps imply within-country risk.

Captions and methods state that maps show country-level reported incidence,
predictions, and uncertainty only. No within-country precision or causal
spatial effect is claimed.
